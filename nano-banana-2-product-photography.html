How to use Nano Banana for product photos: 5 steps and 10 prompts tested on a real product (2026)

By Ted · Published 2026-10-28 · Last updated 2026-10-30

Who runs this: Ted. Ted works at Uneedcomms, the company that makes ModoAI (Modo AI). ModoAI is one of the tools covered in this lab, and every tool, ModoAI included, is scored with the same rules. How tests are run.

How do you use Nano Banana for product photos?

Open the Gemini app, attach one clear photo of your product, and write the scene, light and framing in plain sentences, naming the parts that must not change. Check the first image against your photo before you edit. In our test, Nano Banana 2 followed scenes well but redrew the bottle in 10 of 10 runs.

Step 1: Which photo should you upload?

Use one clear, well-lit photo where the whole product and its small parts are visible. Use the same file every time so results stay comparable. In this test we used one 1600 × 2400 px JPEG of an unlabeled amber dropper bottle, attached in a new chat for every prompt.

Step 2: How should you write the prompt?

Write the scene, the light, the framing and the aspect ratio in plain sentences. Then name the parts that must not change and say what must not be added, for example: “Keep the bottle exactly as in the photo: same shape, amber glass, black ribbed dropper cap and glass pipette. Do not add a label, text or logo.” All 10 prompts below follow this pattern. In our runs this kept the color and stopped added text, but not the exact shape (see Step 4).

Step 3: How do you check the result against your photo?

Put the first image next to your photo and check shape and proportions, small parts, color, any invented text and any watermark. In our 10 runs the amber color stayed true 10 of 10 times and no unrequested text was added, but the bottle shape changed in 10 of 10 runs and a small Gemini sparkle mark showed in 8 of 10 images.

Step 4: Can one edit message fix a changed product?

Not in our test. We replied in the same chat with one restore message that names the parts and says to keep everything else, the exact wording below:

E1 Restore the product: Keep everything else the same, but make the bottle match the attached original photo exactly: same shape, black ribbed dropper cap, glass pipette and amber glass color.

In 10 runs on 2026-10-30 (new chat per prompt, first image, then this one message), Nano Banana 2 returned almost the same image every time: 0 of 10 bottles were restored, and the product fidelity score stayed at 2 of 5 before and after the edit in all 10 runs. So name the parts that must not change anyway, because it kept the amber color and stopped added text, but do not count on an edit to bring back the exact shape. If the exact product matters, check every image against your photo before you use it. On the same 10 prompts, ChatGPT Free kept the bottle closer (fidelity 4 of 5).

Step 5: How do you get the right size for your store?

Ask for the aspect ratio in the prompt. Every ratio we asked for came back: 1:1 (1024 × 1024), 4:5 (825 × 1024), 3:4 (765 × 1024) and 9:16 (572 × 1024), measured on images saved from the app view. Google says Nano Banana 2 can output from 512px up to 4K; we did not check the app's full-size download, so compare the saved size with your store's image rules before uploading.

Is Nano Banana 2 free to use for product photos?

Yes, with limits. Google's Gemini Apps help page lists image generation with Nano Banana 2 as available without a Google AI plan (checked October 27, 2026). Limits are compute-based, and Google notes that image, music and video requests use much more of your allowance than short text chats. Paid Google AI plans add the option to redo images with Nano Banana Pro.

How was the Nano Banana 2 test run?

  • Gemini app on the web with a Google Workspace business account (Pro tier shown), run on October 27, 2026. A new chat for every prompt, the same input file attached each time.
  • Each prompt was pasted word for word. The first image returned is the one scored. No rerolls were used to pick a better one.
  • Up to two follow-up edits were allowed, chosen from three fixed edit messages (listed below). None were used in this round.
  • Time is the seconds from pressing send to the first image, read from page timestamps. Two runs were not timed.
  • Images were saved from the app's result view. Gemini's full-size download was not checked, so the sizes below are the in-app images.
  • Each final image is scored 1 to 5 on five criteria, for a total out of 25. The full rubric is on the methodology page.

  • Scene control was strong: 7 of 10 runs scored 5 of 5 on prompt adherence and the other 3 scored 4, and totals ranged from 16 to 18 out of 25.
  • Product fidelity was the weak point: in all 10 runs the bottle was redrawn as a generic dropper bottle, with a shorter ribbed collar, a thinner pipette and different body proportions, so every run scored 2 of 5 on fidelity.
  • No run added unrequested text or logos, and the amber color stayed true in all 10, including the orange set in prompt 5.

  • On the same 10 prompts, ChatGPT Free kept the bottle much closer to the input (fidelity 4 of 5 in all 10 runs); see the ChatGPT page.

Which other edit messages does this test allow?

Besides E1 above, two more fixed edit messages are allowed, so edits stay comparable across tools:

  • E2 Remove invented marks: Remove any text, label or logo that you added to the bottle. Change nothing else.
  • E3 One targeted change: Free-form, one change only (for example lighting or a prop). The exact wording is recorded with the result.

What are the limits of this test?

  • One product, one input photo. Results on fabric, food or matte packaging can differ.
  • One scored run per prompt per tool. Image models are random, so a second run can score higher or lower. Where a re-run was done, it is listed in the update log.
  • One person scores every image (Ted), using the written rubric on the methodology page. Scores are a documented judgment, not a lab measurement.
  • Accounts differed in the first round: Nano Banana 2 ran on a Google Workspace business account (Pro tier shown) and ChatGPT on the Free plan.
  • Tests are run from South Korea, in English. Limits and models can differ by country and account.
  • Tools change often. Every result carries its run date and the model name the app showed on that day.
